How you show up in search and AI

When someone asks Google for a painter near Greystanes, do you come up? On the map, yes. We searched house painters Greystanes NSW on 14 October 2026 from a signed out browser and Tidy Painting Services was the first listing.

AI assistants are a different story. On 14 October 2026 we asked an AI search assistant (Claude with web search) for reviews of Tidy Painting in Greystanes. It could not find you, and offered a different business with a similar name in Chatswood instead.

That was one test on one day and AI answers change. But the reason is fixable. Assistants read websites, directories and reviews. Your site names no suburbs, shows one phone number while Google shows another, and your business name appears three ways: Tidy Painting on the site, Tidy Painting Services on Google, and A Tidy Painting Services in your terms.

On Bing your own website was the first result for Tidy Painting Sydney. On DuckDuckGo five directory sites sat above your own website for Tidy Painting Greystanes. Google's normal search results could not be checked from our tool on the day.

Under the hood, technical
  • Your enquiry count is not real: the Google tag on your site records a conversion the moment the Contact page loads. If you run Google Ads, Google is being told every visit is a lead, including the phone visitors who cannot send the form. Your reports can look healthy while enquiries go missing.
  • Your email is watched but not protected: your domain asks mail providers to report fake emails, but not to block them. Someone could still send an email that looks like it came from you, and your real quotes are more likely to land in spam.
  • Slow on a phone: in our test on 14 October 2026 the main picture took 6.7 seconds to appear and the page took about 10 seconds to be fully usable. Google's own target for that picture is 2.5 seconds.
  • What Google is told about you is thin: the hidden business card on your site lists your mobile and not the office number, with no street address, no hours and no rating.
  • Small access slips: the picture slider has no label for screen readers and its dots are too small to tap comfortably. You still scored 93 out of 100 on access, which is good.
  • The good news: the site is secure, nothing sensitive is exposed, Google can crawl every page, there is a clean site map, visitor tracking is installed, and your domain lists who is allowed to send your email.
